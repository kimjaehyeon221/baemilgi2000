# BAEMILGI 2000 — Training Chapters

## Why belt colours appear in the app

BAEMILGI 2000 uses martial-arts material culture as a visual language. The five training chapters borrow the familiar adult Brazilian jiu-jitsu belt colour sequence:

1. WHITE — Quest 001–100 — FOUNDATION
2. BLUE — Quest 101–130 — RHYTHM
3. PURPLE — Quest 131–150 — VOLUME
4. BROWN — Quest 151–175 — ENDURANCE
5. BLACK — Quest 176–200 — 2000

These colours are **not** official judo or jiu-jitsu ranks, promotions, certifications, or skill claims. They are app-specific visual chapters mapped onto the five progression ranges already used by the product.

Judo belt systems differ by federation/country and include different coloured kyu progressions. The app therefore must never tell the user that completing a chapter is equivalent to receiving a martial-arts belt.

## Why martial-arts language fits Baemilgi

Baemilgi is also commonly described as the Hindu push-up. Korean judo home-training material has used the movement as a conditioning exercise, describing it as a movement that trains the chest, triceps, shoulders and core.

The product may explain this connection so a new user understands why gi, belt, dojo and training-log language appears in the interface. It must not imply that BAEMILGI 2000 is an official programme of a judo or jiu-jitsu federation.

## UI rule

The chapter colour is a **quiet structural accent**, not a theme takeover.

- Gi White remains the default canvas.
- Black remains the active Challenge/Training canvas.
- The current chapter colour appears in the belt ribbon, progression marker, small metadata and chapter guide.
- Red remains reserved for the CLEARED archival stamp.
- Large target numbers stay unboxed and high-contrast.
- Belt colours must not turn the interface into a game-rarity or XP system.

## Source notes

Reference when this rationale is revisited:

- IBJJF adult competition divisions use White, Blue, Purple, Brown and Black belt categories.
- IJF documentation describes judo kyu colour-belt systems separately and notes historical/national variation.
- Korean National Health Insurance judo home-training material includes Baemilgi/Hindu push-up as a conditioning movement.
